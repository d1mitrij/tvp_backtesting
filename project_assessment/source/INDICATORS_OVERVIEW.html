Indicators Overview — MDB Impact Measurement Frameworks

Frameworks covered: EBRD · IFC/AIMM · FAST-Infra · ADB · AIIB · IDB · IsDB · NDB · AfDB · HIPSO

How to read this document: Each section describes one MDB's measurement framework — how it scores projects, what it measures, and which specific indicators it uses. A cross-framework comparison table at the end shows how the same themes (climate, jobs, gender, water, private capital) are measured differently across institutions.

1. EBRD — Assessment of Transition Qualities (ATQ) / TOMS

Full framework name: Transition Objectives Measurement System / Transition Impact Monitoring System (TOMS/TIMS) Country-level scoring: ATQ scores (1–10 scale, where 10 = best-practice frontier) Project scoring: Expected Transition Impact (ETI), 0–100 (derived from ATQ gap × project intensity)

How the scoring works

The EBRD measures how far each country has advanced toward a sustainable market economy across six Transition Qualities. Each quality is scored 1–10 by normalizing raw indicators to a frontier value (set at the 98th percentile or theoretical optimum). Project ETI scores are higher when a project targets a quality where the host country is furthest behind — i.e., the ATQ gap is largest.

The Six Transition Qualities and Their Indicators

1. Competitive — Are markets open and generating value?

Sub-dimension Key Indicators (unit)
Market structures Applied tariff rates (weighted avg %); SME index (1–10); competition law enforcement (1–10); new business entries (per 1,000 population); subsidies (% of GDP)
Value generation Labour productivity (GDP PPP per worker); Economic Complexity Index; Logistics Performance Index (1–5); credit to private sector (% GDP); global value chain participation (%)

2. Well-Governed — Are institutions transparent and rule-based?

Sub-dimension Key Indicators (unit)
National governance (75%) Regulatory quality (−2.5 to +2.5); government effectiveness (−2.5 to +2.5); Corruption Perception Index (0–100); budget transparency (0–100); press freedom index (0–100); property rights protection (1–7); informality (%)
Corporate governance (25%) Board structure & functioning (0–4); transparency & disclosure (0–5); shareholder rights (0–4); auditing standards quality (1–7)

3. Green — Is the economy decarbonizing and adapting?

Sub-dimension Weight Key Indicators (unit)
Mitigation — physical 37% Renewable electricity share (%); industrial GVA per tCO2; MWh generated per tCO2 emitted; agriculture GVA per tGHG
Mitigation — structural 63% Renewable energy support policy (0–1); carbon price (0–1); fossil fuel subsidies (% GDP); just transition plan (0–1); INDC ambition rating (0–1)
Adaptation — physical 45% NDGAIN human habitat score; water stress index; projected cereal yield change (%); people affected by droughts/floods per 100,000
Adaptation — structural 55% National adaptation plans (0–1); institutional governance quality; agricultural capacity score
Other environment — PM2.5 exposure (μg/m³); waste intensity (kg per US$ household expenditure); threatened species share (%)

4. Inclusive — Are economic benefits broadly shared?

Sub-dimension Key Indicators (unit)
Human capital (33%) Human Capital Index (0–1); labour force participation (%); gender participation gap (women/men ratio); youth NEET rate (%); ICT skills (% population ≥15)
Access to finance (33%) Savings at institutions (% population ≥15); borrowing access (%); fixed broadband penetration (%); safe drinking water access (%); sanitation access (%)
Policies & norms (33%) Social benefit spending (% GDP); non-discrimination index (0–1); Women's Business and the Law score (0–100); gender violence rate (% female population)

5. Resilient — Are the financial system and energy sector stable?

Sub-dimension Weight Key Indicators (unit)
Energy sector (30%) Sector restructuring (0–0.67); private participation (0–0.67); tariff reform (0–0.67); cross-border electricity connectivity (0–0.67); regulator independence (0–0.67)
Banking health (35%) Capital adequacy ratio (%); non-performing loans (%); loan loss reserves (%); foreign currency loans (%); five-bank asset concentration (%)
Alternative financing (25%) Non-bank financial assets (% GDP); market capitalization (0–100%); IPO activity; bond market diversity (0–6.75); money market quality (0–1)
Regulation & safety nets (10%) Deposit insurance effectiveness (1–10); bank risk management quality (1–10); supervisor independence (1–10)

6. Integrated — Is the country connected internally and to global markets?

Sub-dimension Key Indicators (unit)
External integration Total trade (% GDP, 5-year avg); FDI net inflows (% GDP); financial openness (Chinn-Ito index); bilateral investment agreements (#); FDI restrictiveness (0–1)
Internal transport Road connectivity; logistics competence (1–5); tracking capability (1–5); shipment timeliness (1–5); product loss rate (%)
Energy & digital Electricity supply quality (1–7); transmission losses (%); broadband subscriptions per 100; internet users (%); 4G coverage (%)

Sectoral Context (drivers of indicator selection)

Sector Transition challenges informing ETI
Energy High carbon intensity; renewable investment gap; grid modernization lag
Industry Hard-to-abate emissions (steel, cement, chemicals); resource inefficiency
Agrifood Climate exposure; food loss in logistics; biodiversity/food security tension

Unique feature: Country ATQ gap is the multiplier — a project in a low-scoring country on a weak quality earns a higher ETI, incentivizing investment where transition impact is greatest.

2. IFC — AIMM (Anticipated Impact Measurement and Monitoring)

Framework: AIMM General Guidance Note + 20+ Sector Framework Briefs (SFBs) Scoring formula: Total AIMM Score = (Project Outcome Score × Likelihood) + (Market Creation Score × Likelihood) Scale: 0–100 → Excellent (86–100) / Good (51–85) / Satisfactory (31–50) / Low (0–30)

How the scoring works

Each project is scored on two dimensions:

  1. Project Outcomes — direct benefits to people (access, quality, affordability, jobs, GHG)
  2. Market Creation — systemic changes that outlast the project (market structure shifts, replication by others)

Both dimensions are scored for intensity (Below Average → Significantly Above Average, normalized per $1M invested) and then discounted by a likelihood factor (project risk).

3. FAST-Infra — Sustainable Infrastructure Label

Framework: FAST-Infra Sustainable Infrastructure® Label Structure: 4 Dimensions · 14 Criteria · ~220 indicators (unique alphanumeric IDs) Label types: Self-Assessed (ops: valid 24 months, refresh every 12 months) | Verified (valid 36 months, annual surveillance audit)

How the scoring works

FAST-Infra uses a universal indicator set for all infrastructure sectors. Each indicator has a permanent alphanumeric ID. Two indicator types drive label eligibility: Baseline Requirements (all 14 criteria must be met) and Positive Contributions (at least one criterion must show a measurable contribution beyond baseline). Time-series indicators use .A (historical) and .B (forecast) suffixes, enabling longitudinal tracking. Projects additionally must satisfy Minimum Safeguards before any criterion is scored.

5. AIIB — Results Monitoring Framework (RMF)

Framework: Results Architecture — two-level (Project + Corporate) Project rating: 4-point scale: Highly Satisfactory / Satisfactory / Moderately Unsatisfactory / Highly Unsatisfactory Evaluation criteria: 6 OECD-DAC criteria + AIIB Work Quality (7th criterion) Climate target: ≥50% of financing as climate finance by 2025

How the scoring works

At the project level, each project defines Project Development Objectives (PDOs, i.e., outcomes) and Intermediate Results Indicators (IRIs, i.e., outputs). Progress is tracked at three points: ex-ante (appraisal), mid-term, and completion. At the corporate level, AIIB tracks three elements: portfolio volume, portfolio alignment with strategic targets, and portfolio performance.

9. AfDB — ADOA (Additionality and Development Outcomes Assessment) 3.0

Framework: ADOA 3.0 — applies to all Non-Sovereign Operations (NSO) of the African Development Bank Scoring scale: 4-point qualitative scale (Excellent / Good / Marginal / Poor) mapped to a continuous 1–10 score Two main dimensions: Development Outcomes + Additionality (DFI value-add)

How the scoring works

Each project is rated across two top-level dimensions. Development Outcomes measures the actual benefits generated for people, markets, and the environment. Additionality measures what the AfDB contributes that would not have happened without its involvement (financial and non-financial). Both dimensions are likelihood-adjusted for country and project context risk. A project in a highly constrained environment (rated on a 3-point context scale) is judged more favourably than the same project in an easy environment.

Context scale: Highly Constrained → Constrained → Unconstrained Outcome categories rated: 6–7 categories depending on sector, each scored Excellent / Good / Marginal / Poor

Development Outcomes Dimension

Outcome Category Key Indicators
Access to financial services New accounts opened (#); loans to MSMEs ($M/unit); insurance policies written (#)
Access to goods & services Housing units financed (#); transport connections improved; utilities connected (#)
Private sector development Firms supported (#); market entrants (#); investment catalyzed ($M); jobs created (#)
Household outcomes Beneficiary income improvement (%); poverty reduction contribution; livelihood diversification
Resilience & adaptation People with improved climate resilience (#); assets protected; adaptation investment ($M)
Environment & climate GHG avoided/reduced (tCO2e/year); renewable capacity added (MW); land restored (ha)
Regional integration Cross-border infrastructure projects (#); trade facilitation impact; regional connectivity improvement

Additionality Dimension

Additionality Type What it measures
Financial additionality Whether project financing terms (tenor, risk, pricing) fill a genuine market gap; mobilization of co-investors ($M)
Non-financial additionality Technical assistance provided; knowledge transfer; governance/standards improvements; reputational signalling
Political risk mitigation AfDB participation reducing sovereign/regulatory risk perception for private investors

Sector-Specific Frameworks

ADOA 3.0 includes dedicated indicator catalogues for 7 sectors:

Sector Representative Sector-Specific Indicators
Financial Services Portfolio-at-risk ratio; deposit mobilization; financial inclusion index change
Energy Levelized cost of energy (LCOE); SAIDI/SAIFI reliability; electrification rate change (%)
Transport Vehicle operating cost reduction (%); freight volume (t/year); road safety fatalities per 100km
Water Non-revenue water reduction (%); water quality compliance rate (%); coverage expansion (km of pipes)
Agriculture Smallholder yield improvement (t/ha); post-harvest losses reduction (%); market price volatility reduction
Manufacturing Capacity utilization (%); export volume growth; resource efficiency improvement
Digital Infrastructure Broadband penetration increase (%); digital services adoption (#); latency reduction (ms)

Unique features:

  • Continuous 1–10 scoring replaces older 6-point discrete scale, enabling more nuanced differentiation
  • Explicit context-risk adjustment: identical outcomes in a "Highly Constrained" country earn a higher score
  • Integrated theory-of-change assessment — evaluators must trace the causal pathway from project to outcome
  • Regional integration is a standalone outcome category (important for pan-African mandate)
  • ADOA-RO 2.0 sub-framework for Regional Operations applies additional cross-border integration criteria

10. HIPSO — Harmonized Indicators for Private Sector Operations

Framework: HIPSO (IFI Partnership) Structure: Sector indicator catalogues + cross-cutting thematic indicators Coverage: 14 sectors + cross-cutting categories (Gender, Jobs, Environment, Community) · ~177 indicators Type: Voluntary harmonized definitions adopted by IFC, EBRD, AfDB, ADB, IDB, EIB, IsDB, and others for private sector operations reporting

How it works

HIPSO is a joint initiative of the International Finance Institution Partnership that defines a common set of output and outcome indicators for private sector investment operations. Unlike AIMM or TOMS, HIPSO defines what to measure, not how to score it. The goal is cross-MDB comparability: institutions use the same indicator IDs and definitions so portfolios can be compared. All indicators are mapped to relevant UN SDG targets.
