## 1. EBRD — Assessment of Transition Qualities (ATQ) / TOMS

**Full framework name:** Transition Objectives Measurement System / Transition Impact Monitoring System (TOMS/TIMS)
**Country-level scoring:** ATQ scores (-10% to +10% scale relative to frontier gap).
**Project scoring:** Expected Transition Impact (ETI), derived from ATQ gap × project intensity, including strategic uplifts.

### How the scoring works
The EBRD measures how far each country has advanced toward a sustainable market economy across **six Transition Qualities**: Competitive, Well-governed, Green, Inclusive, Resilient, and Integrated. 

**Pathway to ETI generation:**
1. **Choice of Transition Quality:** Typically two qualities are selected per project.
2. **Base Score:** A questionnaire assesses the ambition level of the impact objectives.
3. **Gap Adjustment:** The score is adjusted to reflect the country context using ATQ indicators (a project in a country with a high transition gap receives a higher adjustment).
4. **Strategic Uplifts:** Additive elements based on strategic relevance (e.g., local currency/equity uplift).
5. **Final ETI Score:** Results in the final Expected Transition Impact rating.

### Theory of Change Pathway
*   **Outputs:** Physical capacity, products/processes, financial intermediation.
*   **Stakeholder Outcomes:** Improved financial performance, environmental benefits, changes in practices.
*   **Channels of Systemic Change:** Demonstration and replication, innovation and competition, capacity building, market enabling.

### The Six Transition Qualities and Their Indicators (Deep Dive: Competitive)

**1. Competitive** — Are markets open and generating value?
The "Competitive" quality evaluates if markets are building dynamic and open structures that stimulate competition, entrepreneurship, and productivity growth.

*   **Sub-dimension: Market structures (50% weight)**
    *   Applied tariff rates (weighted average) [14%] - *Frontier: Georgia*
    *   Subsidies expense (% of GDP) [14%] - *Frontier: Tajikistan*
    *   Resolving insolvency score [14%] - *Frontier: US*
    *   Number of new business entries (scaled by pop.) [14%] - *Frontier: Estonia*
    *   SME index adjusted (1-10) [14%] - *Frontier: UK*
    *   Competition Law, Institutions, and Enforcement index [14%] - *Frontier: UK*
    *   Business services exports (% of GDP) [14%] - *Frontier: Estonia*

*   **Sub-dimension: Capacity to generate value added (50% weight)**
    *   Economic Complexity Index [14%] - *Frontier: Japan*
    *   Knowledge economy index (KEI) adjusted [14%] - *Frontier: Sweden*
    *   Logistics Performance Index (1-5) [14%] - *Frontier: Germany*
    *   Skills (WEF Competitiveness Index) [14%] - *Frontier: Germany*
    *   Labour productivity (output per worker) [14%] - *Frontier: US*
    *   Credit to private sector (% of GDP) [14%] - *Frontier: US*
    *   Global value chain participation [14%] - *Frontier: Slovak Republic*

*(Other qualities include Well-Governed, Green, Inclusive, Resilient, and Integrated, focusing on regulatory quality, decarbonization, human capital, financial stability, and trade connectivity respectively).*

## 2. IFC — AIMM (Anticipated Impact Measurement and Monitoring)

**Framework:** AIMM General Guidance Note + 20+ Sector Framework Briefs (SFBs)
**Scoring formula:** Total AIMM Score = (Project Outcome Score × Risk Factor) + (Market Outcome Score × Risk Factor)

### How the scoring works
The AIMM rating system is fully integrated into IFC's investment approval process. It brings three critical elements under a single impact management umbrella: end-to-end system monitoring, decision-making, and reporting.

Each project is scored based on two pillars:
1. **Project Outcomes:** Direct, indirect, and induced effects on stakeholders (employees, customers, suppliers), the economy-wide effects, and environmental effects.
2. **Market Outcomes:** The project's potential for generating systemic, sector-wide changes that enhance market **Competitiveness, Resilience, and Sustainability**.

### 2025/2026 System Enhancements
To keep the framework relevant to evolving business needs, IFC implemented key enhancements:
1. **Transparent Market Impacts:** Projects' expected market impact is now driven by an assessment of their levels of **innovation and scalability** (strongest impact when both are high).
2. **Simplicity:** The scoring grid was simplified by using two levels of risk, replacing the previous complex structure that included "full potential" and three likelihood levels.
3. **Strategic Alignment:** Transparent incentives were created across all sectors for projects that are highly impactful in their contribution to **climate and inclusion** objectives (World Bank Group priorities).
4. **Portfolio Monitoring:** Enhanced emphasis on comparing on-going actual results with ex-ante expectations to promptly engage clients and derive lessons learned.

### Sector-Specific Transition Indicators (Examples)

*   **Agribusiness:**
    *   *Gap:* Farmer's livelihood; Yield Gap; Agriculture labor productivity.
    *   *Intensity:* Farmers reached (#); Share of female farmers; Percentage increase in yields.
*   **Climate Mitigation (Cross-cutting):**
    *   *Gap:* Sector share of country emissions; GHG intensity growth rate.
    *   *Intensity:* Carbon intensity (tCO2e per unit); Energy intensity (KWh/MJ per unit).
*   **Manufacturing:**
    *   *Gap:* Efficiency of product distribution network.
    *   *Intensity:* Percent increase in number of distributors; Change in time needed for consumers to receive product.

## 3. FAST-Infra — Sustainable Infrastructure Label

**Framework:** FAST-Infra Sustainable Infrastructure Label
**Structure:** 4 Dimensions · 14 Criteria · ~220 indicators.

**How the scoring works:**
Uses a universal indicator set for all infrastructure sectors. Driven by label eligibility: **Baseline Requirements** (all 14 criteria must be met, primarily aligned with IFC Performance Standards) and **Positive Contributions** (at least one criterion must show a measurable contribution beyond baseline).

*   *Environmental:* Biodiversity, Climate Mitigation, Circular Economy, Pollution.
*   *Social:* Inclusivity, Health & Safety, Human Rights, Land Resettlement, Stakeholder Engagement.
*   *Governance:* Anti-Corruption, Transparency, Fiscal Integrity, Compliance.
*   *Resilience:* Climate Adaptation.

## 4. HIPSO — Harmonized Indicators for Private Sector Operations

**Framework:** HIPSO (IFI Partnership)
**Structure:** Sector indicator catalogues + cross-cutting thematic indicators.

**How it works:**
HIPSO defines a common set of output and outcome indicators for private sector investment operations. Unlike AIMM or TOMS, HIPSO defines *what* to measure, not how to score it. 

*   *Energy:* Renewable Energy Generated (MWh); New Connections (#).
*   *Jobs:* Direct Jobs Supported (FTE); Female Direct Jobs.
*   *Climate:* GHG Emissions (tCO2e/year); GHG Sequestration.
*   *Finance:* Loans Outstanding; Value of Loans to Women/MSMEs.

## 5. Other Frameworks (Summary)

*   **ADB (Design and Monitoring Framework):** Logic chain aligned with Strategy 2030. Uses 4-point scale for post-evaluation (Relevance, Effectiveness, Efficiency, Impact, Sustainability).
*   **AIIB (Results Monitoring Framework):** Two-level tracking (Project + Corporate). Project rating on a 4-point scale. Corporate tracking includes climate finance share (≥50% by 2025).
*   **IDB (2024–2030 Impact Framework):** Three pillars: Social Progress, Economic Growth, Climate Action.
*   **IsDB (Realigned Strategy 2023–2025):** Integrates standard development indicators with *Maqasid al-Shari'ah* (Islamic Ethical Layer, e.g., Protection of Life, Wealth, Progeny).
*   **NDB (General Strategy 2022–2026):** Focuses exclusively on sustainable infrastructure in BRICS+ economies.
*   **AfDB (ADOA 3.0):** Scores non-sovereign operations on Development Outcomes + Additionality (DFI value-add), adjusted for country context risk.
